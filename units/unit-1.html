Moles and Molar Mass

The mole is a fundamental unit used in chemistry to describe an amount of substance. It connects the microscopic world of particles to measurable quantities such as mass.

What Is a Mole?

A mole is a counting unit, much like a dozen. While one dozen represents 12 objects, one mole represents 6.022 × 1023 particles.

Avogadro’s number: One mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 1023 representative particles.

Representative particles may be atoms, molecules, ions, or formula units, depending on the substance.

1 mol = 6.022 × 1023 particles

Converting Between Moles and Particles

To convert from moles to particles, multiply by Avogadro’s number. To convert from particles to moles, divide by Avogadro’s number.

Example: Moles to Particles

How many molecules are present in 2.00 moles of H2O?

2.00 mol × 6.022 × 1023 molecules 1 mol = 1.20 × 1024 molecules

What Is Molar Mass?

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance. It is measured in grams per mole, or g/mol. The molar mass of an element is numerically equal to its atomic mass on the periodic table.

Example: Finding Molar Mass

Calculate the molar mass of H2O.

2(1.008 g/mol) + 16.00 g/mol = 18.02 g/mol

Converting Between Grams and Moles

Molar mass is used as a conversion factor between grams and moles. To convert grams to moles, divide by the molar mass. To convert moles to grams, multiply by the molar mass.

Moles = Mass in grams ÷ Molar mass

Example: Grams to Moles

How many moles are present in 36.04 grams of H2O?

The molar mass of H2O is 18.02 g/mol.

36.04 g × 1 mol H2O 18.02 g H2O = 2.000 mol H2O

Practice Questions

Try each problem before opening the solution.

Question 1

How many molecules are present in 0.500 mol of CO2?

Show solution

Multiply the number of moles by Avogadro’s number:

0.500 mol × 6.022 × 1023 molecules 1 mol = 3.01 × 1023 molecules

Question 2

How many moles are present in 44.01 g of CO2?

Show solution

The molar mass of CO2 is 44.01 g/mol.

44.01 g × 1 mol 44.01 g = 1.000 mol CO2

Question 3

What mass of Na contains 3.011 × 1023 atoms?

Show solution

First, convert atoms to moles:

3.011 × 1023 atoms × 1 mol 6.022 × 1023 atoms = 0.5000 mol Na

Next, multiply by the molar mass of sodium:

0.5000 mol Na × 22.99 g Na 1 mol Na = 11.50 g Na

Mass Spectrometry

Mass spectrometry is an experimental technique used to identify the isotopes of an element and measure their relative abundances. The resulting data can be displayed in a mass spectrum.

Key idea: A mass spectrum provides evidence about which isotopes are present in a sample and how common each isotope is.

Reading a Mass Spectrum

A mass spectrum contains peaks representing particles detected by the instrument. For the simple atomic ions studied here, the horizontal axis shows the mass-to-charge ratio, written as m/z, and the vertical axis shows relative abundance.

When an ion has a charge of +1, its mass-to-charge ratio is numerically equal to its approximate isotopic mass. Therefore, a peak at m/z = 62 can represent an isotope with an approximate mass of 62 amu.

Interpreting peaks: The location of a peak helps identify an isotope, while the height of the peak indicates its relative abundance.

Calculating Average Atomic Mass

The atomic mass displayed on the periodic table is a weighted average of the naturally occurring isotopes of an element. More abundant isotopes contribute more heavily to this average.

Average atomic mass = Σ(isotopic mass × fractional abundance)

The symbol Σ means to add the contribution from every isotope. Before calculating, convert each percentage abundance into a decimal.

Example: Finding Average Atomic Mass

A hypothetical element has two naturally occurring isotopes. The first has a mass of 62 amu and an abundance of 69.0%. The second has a mass of 64 amu and an abundance of 31.0%. Calculate the element’s average atomic mass.

(62 amu × 0.690) + (64 amu × 0.310) = 62.62 amu

The average is closer to 62 amu because the isotope with a mass of 62 amu is more abundant.
